The 7/7 testing standard

A buyer's checklist, not a certification we issue. When evaluating a research peptide vendor, these are the seven independent lab-testing categories worth confirming. We track whether vendors publish this documentation; we do not perform the testing ourselves. Always review current certificates directly with each vendor.

1. Net purity

The percentage of the vial that is the target peptide, typically measured by HPLC. Higher purity means fewer unknown byproducts. Ask for a recent HPLC report tied to the batch you are buying.

2. Net content

Whether the vial actually contains the labeled amount, for example a 10mg vial holding 10mg. Verified by mass balance or quantitative analysis. Under-filled vials are a common quality gap.

3. Heavy metals

Screening for contaminants such as lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury that can carry over from synthesis. Reported against defined safety limits.

4. Endotoxins

Bacterial endotoxin testing (LAL), relevant to anything intended for injection in research settings. Reported in EU/mg against a threshold.

5. Identification

Confirmation that the compound is what the label says, usually by mass spectrometry. Distinguishes the correct peptide from a mislabeled or substituted one.

6. Sterility

Testing for microbial contamination. Most relevant for lyophilized products and reconstitution workflows in a research context.

7. Conformity

An overall check that the batch conforms to its specification across the categories above, tying the individual reports together for a given lot.

How we use this. Inclusion on MyPeptidePrice.com reflects that a vendor's pricing is trackable, not that they meet all seven standards. Where a vendor publishes testing documentation we note it, but we do not test products ourselves and make no representation about any vendor's results. This framework is provided so you can ask the right questions and review the actual certificates before deciding.
